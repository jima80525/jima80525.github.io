Djangonaut Space - Captain's Log
Sun 22 November 2026 Posts

I recently joined the Djangonaut Space program in the "captain" role for team Pyxis. For those who don't know, Dangonaut Space is a program designed to develop leaders in the open source and in particular, the Django community. The program runs in eight week sessions, with teams of developers working together on project. In this session, session 8, the projects range from Beeware, and Django itself, to the DjangoGirls website, which is what our team is working on.

In addition to the fabulous organizers who setup and keep things running for the program as a whole, each team has two volunteers to help the Djangonauts grow their skills. The two roles are called Navigator and Captain.

The Navigator role is generally someone deeply involved in the project. In our case, Anna has been a long-time contributor to the DjangoGirls project and is a maintainer for the codebase. She provides technical and project-specific guidance for the team; helping them set up PR and run the process through to merging.

My role, Captain, is much more of a cheerleader, sounding board, and mental health facilitator. The captain's responsibility is to make sure all Djangonauts feel welcomed, encouraged, and find a way to contribute that fits with their lifestyle, commitment, and abilities.

This session I'm the captain for Team Pyxis (team names are constellations this session) working on the DjangoGirls website. We have three members and they're doing great. We've been cleaning up the onboarding documentation for developers and fixing bugs in the site's code base. It's rewarding to see the progress that we're making as a team.

I'm in an interesting position here, as I'm not really much of a Django developer or even user. While it may seem odd that I would be interested and that they would be welcoming for me to take a role, there are solid reasons why I'm here - and happy to be so.

I wanted to become involved in the program for several reasons, the biggest one being that I've found that many of the friends in the Python community are deeply involved in Django. I'm lucky enough to have met and hung out with several amazing people involved in the DSF, DEFNA, and Djangonaut Space programs. The energy, joy, and commitment of these people are inspiring. That's the type of people I want to associate with. As I move into retirement, I'm looking for ways to use my experience to help in projects like this.

Now, why they were interested in me is a different story. I certainly am not a good candidate for the Navigator role, as I'm currently not a maintainer or deeply embedded in any of the projects related to Django. The captain role, is far less technical and far more "political", for lack of a better term.

While I can and do help out with technical issues, much of my role description here is making sure the djangonauts feel welcomed and that they have another resource to turn to if they're having difficulties. I've been doing 1-on-1 meetings with each of them, some of them every week, some every other. These have given us a chance to really connect and discuss how open source contribution can fit into their lives.

I've loved my involvement with this team and this program so far and am so lucky to be in a position where I can do this!

- Jim Anderson
